Working papers

Affine Transfer for Conditional Quantiles with Heavy-Tailed Responses

Kirill Sirik. Senior thesis, Princeton University, 2026.

Abstract

A small target sample may not support a rich quantile regression even when a related source population does. We fit the target quantile as an affine function of a fitted source quantile, principal-component scores, and selected covariates. We prove a finite-sample bound on squared prediction error that separates affine approximation, input estimation, and target estimation errors; under boundedness and density conditions, its target term has order $k \log n / n$ for $k$ coefficients and $n$ target responses, and no response moments are required. When several quantile levels share one slope, we identify, for local scale departures, the boundary where the squared bias of sharing offsets the variance it saves. For three quartiles under normal or $t_3$ errors and $d = 1, 2, 5, 10$ inputs, the 5% joint slope test has limiting power 0.14–0.53 at the outer-quartile boundary, so nonrejection does not establish a prediction advantage for sharing. In 2024 American Community Survey data, transfer from California to the eight least populous states lowers mean held-out check loss at level 0.90 by 16.8–21.6% at $n = 100$ relative to target-only regression with sixteen coefficients. Against target-only regression on the same scores and covariates, without the source prediction, the reduction is 1.9–10.2% and persists at larger $n$.

On Persistence in Congressional Stock Selection

Kirill Sirik. Working paper (junior independent work), Princeton University, 2026.

Abstract

Do the same congressional households repeatedly pick stocks that outperform? For 7,964 purchases by 38 households in 2015–2020 and 2021–2025, persistence is negative under a factor-only benchmark and positive under an event-study benchmark, which carries forward recent alpha; neither estimate is statistically significant. The positive estimate can reflect repeated underperformance: recent alpha barely predicts later returns (forecast coefficient 0.057, where the benchmark assumes one), yet the same households keep buying stocks with high recent alpha. A clustered pooled test can miss an arbitrarily large gain to one household; for a household specified in advance making 50 purchases a year, detecting a 5 percent annual advantage would take about 74 years. Benchmarks carrying recent alpha forward should first be checked against later returns.

Hotel Concentration and Local News Coverage of Short-Term Rentals

Kirill Sirik. Working paper, Princeton University, 2026.

Abstract

Consolidation can strengthen incumbents' incentives to seek restrictions on entrants. I study whether the 2016 hotel acquisitions, chiefly Marriott's purchase of Starwood, changed local newspaper coverage of short-term rentals. Among 326,251 news articles from 613 U.S. cities, a language model identifies 283 about short-term rentals, and this coverage rises sharply when a city first regulates them. Coverage did not grow more where the acquisitions raised hotel concentration more, which gives no indication that consolidation led hotels to make their case against short-term rentals through local newspapers. With controls for pre-merger concentration, size, news volume, and Airbnb presence, the 95 percent confidence intervals rule out increases in overall and negative coverage larger than about a tenth of their post-merger means per 100 HHI points, though the bound is looser for the most exposed cities. Coverage appears to rise in the more exposed cities only when pre-merger concentration is the sole control, and the rise disappears once their larger pre-merger Airbnb presence is held fixed.

Works in progress

Who Reaches the Economics PhD Job Market? Undergraduate Origins, Predoc Pathways, and Institutional Concentration at Top U.S. Programs

Kirill Sirik. Work in progress, Princeton University, 2026.

Studies the undergraduate institutions, master's programs, predoc positions, and research jobs that precede doctoral training in economics, building a source-linked dataset on job-market candidates at highly ranked U.S. economics departments from 2015–16 through 2024–25.

Preliminary abstract

This project constructs a new longitudinal dataset describing the educational and professional pathways of economics PhD job-market candidates at highly ranked U.S. doctoral programs from 2015–16 through 2024–25. The data combine official department rosters, archived job-market pages, public curricula vitae, placement records, and institutional metadata. They document undergraduate and master's institutions, fields of study, degree timing, pre-PhD research employment, predoc experience, doctoral specialization, and first placement. The project examines whether the pipeline into highly ranked economics PhD programs has become increasingly concentrated among a small set of undergraduate institutions and whether the growing use of master's degrees and full-time predoc positions has broadened or narrowed access. It also studies variation across program-ranking bands, domestic and international educational pathways, public and private institutions, and placement outcomes. The project is currently in the data-collection and quality-assurance stage; all reported coverage and findings remain preliminary.

Data collection and validation in progress. Collaboration is welcome; please email me.

Data Centers Will Strain the Grid (Even with Demand Response)

Kirill Sirik, Alex Crosier, Ronnie Sircar. ORFEUS research group, Princeton University, 2025.

Simulates a full year of operation on a synthetic 7,000 bus Texas grid to measure how 22 GW of projected data center load affects wholesale prices, system costs, and reliability, and how far a demand response scheme can offset those effects.
