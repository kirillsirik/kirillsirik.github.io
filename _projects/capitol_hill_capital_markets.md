abstract: >-
  Do the same congressional households repeatedly pick stocks that outperform? For 7,964 purchases by 38 households in
  2015–2020 and 2021–2025, persistence is negative under a factor-only benchmark and positive under an event-study
  benchmark, which carries forward recent alpha; neither estimate is statistically significant. The positive estimate
  can reflect repeated underperformance: recent alpha barely predicts later returns (forecast coefficient 0.057, where
  the benchmark assumes one), yet the same households keep buying stocks with high recent alpha. A clustered pooled
  test can miss an arbitrarily large gain to one household; for a household specified in advance making 50 purchases a
  year, detecting a 5 percent annual advantage would take about 74 years. Benchmarks carrying recent alpha forward
  should first be checked against later returns.
